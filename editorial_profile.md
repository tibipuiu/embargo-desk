# ZME Science editorial profile

Edit this file freely: it is read on every run and steers what the agent recommends.

## Who we write for
Curious general readers, not specialists. Many arrive from search and social media.
Stories must be explainable in plain language without losing accuracy.

## What makes a strong pick
- A genuinely new finding, not an incremental step only specialists would notice.
- A clear "huh, I didn't know that" or "this affects me" moment.
- Topics our readers love: climate and environment, health and medicine, nutrition,
  psychology and the brain, animals and evolution, space and astronomy,
  archaeology and human origins, physics oddities, energy and green tech,
  surprising everyday science.
- Strong visuals available or easy to find (fossils, telescope images, animals, maps).
- Solid evidence: peer-reviewed, decent sample sizes, human data where health claims are made.
- Counterintuitive results, records ("oldest", "largest", "first"), and findings that
  challenge a common belief.

## What to skip or flag
- Institutional news, awards, grants, appointments, events, and product launches.
- Very narrow technical or methods papers with no story for a lay reader.
- Mouse/cell studies sold as human health breakthroughs (can still be a pick, but flag it).
- Purely correlational claims presented as causal.
- Releases that are mostly hype with thin underlying results.

## Tone
Accurate, vivid, a little playful, never sensationalist. Headlines should be specific
and honest; no "scientists baffled" or "could cure cancer".
